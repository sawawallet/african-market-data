# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is acting in good faith and knows something
you do not.

## What is expected

This project takes contributions from people across many countries, working in
several languages, with very different levels of experience in market data.
Someone correcting a venue's trading hours may know that exchange far better
than they know Rust, and that is exactly the contribution the project needs
most. Meet people where they are.

Concretely:

- Critique the change, not the person who wrote it.
- When you say something is wrong, say what would make it right.
- English is a second or third language for many contributors here. Read past
  the phrasing to the point being made.
- If you do not know, say so. A question is cheaper than a wrong assumption
  that reaches production.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Demeaning comments about anyone's
nationality, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexuality, disability, age, or level
of experience. Publishing someone's private information. Sustained disruption
of discussion.

None of this becomes acceptable because a technical point underneath it happens
to be correct.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and any other project
space, and to anyone representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report a problem by emailing the maintainers or opening a private security
advisory on the repository. Reports are handled confidentially. Maintainers who
do not follow or enforce this document in good faith may be removed from the
project.

Enforcement is proportionate: a private word first where that is likely to
work, up to a permanent ban where it is not.
